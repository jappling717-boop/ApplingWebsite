Literature Review

This page includes my course-related book review, key takeaways, and reflection on how the reading connects to AI and construction management.

Reviewed Literature

Overall Topic: AI Adoption in Construction and Facilities

This literature review examines how artificial intelligence is being adopted across the architecture, engineering, construction, and facilities industries. The reviewed studies show that AI is not just a new software trend, but an organizational change that affects project planning, decision-making, risk management, BIM workflows, training, and long-term competitiveness.

Artificial Intelligence in Construction Project Management: A Structured Literature Review of Its Evolution in Application and Future Trends

Authors: Y. Adebayo, P. Udoh, X. B. Kamudyariwa, and O. A. Osobajo

This study explains how AI is being used in construction project management, especially in planning, monitoring, control, forecasting, scheduling, safety monitoring, and decision-support. It shows that the strongest AI applications are those that provide measurable value in real project environments.

Artificial Intelligence (AI)-Based Technology Adoption in the Construction Industry: A Cross-National Perspective Using the Technology Acceptance Model

Authors: S. Na, S. Heo, W. Choi, C. Kim, and S. W. Whang

This article focuses on why construction professionals accept or reject AI-based tools. Using the Technology Acceptance Model, the authors argue that adoption depends on perceived usefulness, ease of use, and how well the technology fits existing work practices. This connects strongly to construction because even powerful tools will not succeed if workers do not trust them or understand how to use them.

Advancing AI-Powered BIM for Circularity in Construction in the UK and Turkiye: State-of-the-Art Review and Capability Maturity Modelling

Authors: T. Omotayo, A. M. Tanyer, J. Deng, A. Kaima, A. Akponeware, M. K. Pekericli, S. Shikder, and M. Ogunnusi

This study connects AI-powered BIM with circular economy goals and organizational maturity. The authors explain that AI adoption is shaped by integration, technology development, government support, education, incentives, industry readiness, and cultural attitudes. This is important because it shows that AI adoption happens in stages and depends on more than simply buying advanced software.

Generative AI in Construction Risk Management: A Bibliometric Analysis of the Associated Benefits and Risks

Authors: M. A. H. Mohamed, M. K. S. Al-Mhdawi, U. Ojiako, N. Dacre, A. Qazi, and F. Rahimian

This article reviews the fast-growing interest in generative AI for construction risk management. It highlights the benefits of improved prediction, adaptive response, decision support, and information processing, while also recognizing concerns related to data security, privacy, performance, compliance, and responsible implementation.

Key Takeaways

  • AI adoption in construction is strongest when it solves practical project problems, such as scheduling, planning, safety monitoring, forecasting, BIM coordination, and decision-support.
  • Successful AI implementation depends on people, not just technology. Workers and organizations must see the tools as useful, easy to use, trustworthy, and compatible with existing construction workflows.
  • AI adoption is a gradual maturity process. Companies need training, leadership, clear policies, data governance, and a willingness to manage risks before AI can become fully embedded in construction and facilities operations.

My Reflection

This literature review helped me understand that AI in construction is not just about using new technology. It is also about how construction companies, facility teams, and project managers adapt to change. The most useful AI tools appear to be the ones that support real project needs, such as improving schedules, tracking risk, organizing project information, and helping teams make better decisions.

As someone interested in construction management, facilities, BIM, and healthcare construction, this topic connects directly to my professional goals. AI could become a valuable tool for project coordinators, project managers, and facilities teams, but only if organizations provide proper training and create clear processes for using it responsibly. The review also showed me that human judgment will still matter. AI may help organize information and identify patterns, but construction professionals will still need to verify information, communicate with teams, understand field conditions, and make responsible decisions.

Overall, I learned that AI adoption should be viewed as both a technology opportunity and a leadership challenge. The companies that benefit most from AI will likely be the ones that combine strong tools with good training, clear policies, and a culture that is open to innovation.
