Literature Review

A research showcase on AI adoption, construction management, BIM, risk, organizational readiness, and facilities-related workflows.

Research Focus

AI Adoption in Construction and Facilities

This literature review examines how artificial intelligence is being adopted across architecture, engineering, construction, and facilities-related industries. The reviewed studies show that AI is not just a software trend. It is an organizational shift that affects planning, decision-making, risk management, BIM workflows, training, and long-term competitiveness.

Main Research Question

How is AI changing construction and facilities workflows, and what factors determine whether organizations successfully adopt these tools?

Key Takeaways

These are the major patterns that appeared across the reviewed research.

🏗️

AI Must Solve Real Project Problems

AI adoption is strongest when tools improve planning, scheduling, safety monitoring, forecasting, BIM coordination, decision-support, or document-heavy workflows.

🤝

Trust and Usability Matter

Construction professionals are more likely to use AI when they see the tool as useful, easy to understand, compatible with existing workflows, and worth trusting.

📈

Adoption Is a Maturity Process

AI implementation requires training, leadership, data governance, policy, and a willingness to manage risk before tools become fully embedded in daily operations.

Reviewed Sources

Each source below is summarized by its main idea, major topic area, and why it matters to construction or facilities management.

Project Management Scheduling Decision-Support

Artificial Intelligence in Construction Project Management: A Structured Literature Review of Its Evolution in Application and Future Trends

Authors: Y. Adebayo, P. Udoh, X. B. Kamudyariwa, and O. A. Osobajo

This study explains how AI is being used in construction project management, especially in planning, monitoring, control, forecasting, scheduling, safety monitoring, and decision-support.

Why it matters:

The strongest AI applications are the ones that create measurable value in real project environments, not just impressive demonstrations.

Adoption Trust Technology Acceptance

Artificial Intelligence (AI)-Based Technology Adoption in the Construction Industry: A Cross-National Perspective Using the Technology Acceptance Model

Authors: S. Na, S. Heo, W. Choi, C. Kim, and S. W. Whang

This article focuses on why construction professionals accept or reject AI-based tools. Using the Technology Acceptance Model, the authors argue that adoption depends on perceived usefulness, ease of use, and fit with existing work practices.

Why it matters:

Even powerful tools will not succeed if workers do not trust them, understand them, or see how they fit into daily construction workflows.

BIM Circularity Organizational Maturity

Advancing AI-Powered BIM for Circularity in Construction in the UK and Turkiye: State-of-the-Art Review and Capability Maturity Modelling

Authors: T. Omotayo, A. M. Tanyer, J. Deng, A. Kaima, A. Akponeware, M. K. Pekericli, S. Shikder, and M. Ogunnusi

This study connects AI-powered BIM with circular economy goals and organizational maturity. The authors explain that adoption is shaped by integration, technology development, government support, education, incentives, industry readiness, and culture.

Why it matters:

AI adoption happens in stages and depends on more than simply buying advanced software. Organizations need readiness, process alignment, and digital maturity.

Risk Management Generative AI Governance

Generative AI in Construction Risk Management: A Bibliometric Analysis of the Associated Benefits and Risks

Authors: M. A. H. Mohamed, M. K. S. Al-Mhdawi, U. Ojiako, N. Dacre, A. Qazi, and F. Rahimian

This article reviews the fast-growing interest in generative AI for construction risk management. It highlights improved prediction, adaptive response, decision support, and information processing, while recognizing concerns related to data security, privacy, performance, compliance, and responsible implementation.

Why it matters:

AI may help project teams better anticipate and manage risk, but responsible use requires governance, privacy protection, and human review.

Overall Synthesis

Across the reviewed studies, AI appears most valuable when it supports existing construction workflows instead of replacing professional judgment. The research points toward a future where AI helps teams organize information, forecast issues, support decisions, and improve coordination.

However, adoption depends heavily on people, process, and leadership. Organizations need clear policies, training, trustworthy tools, data governance, and a practical reason to use AI before the technology can become a normal part of project delivery.

My Reflection

This literature review helped me understand that AI in construction is not just about using new technology. It is also about how construction companies, facility teams, and project managers adapt to change. The most useful AI tools appear to be the ones that support real project needs, such as improving schedules, tracking risk, organizing project information, and helping teams make better decisions.

As someone interested in construction management, facilities, BIM, and healthcare construction, this topic connects directly to my professional goals. AI could become a valuable tool for project coordinators, project managers, and facilities teams, but only if organizations provide proper training and create clear processes for using it responsibly.

Overall, I learned that AI adoption should be viewed as both a technology opportunity and a leadership challenge. The companies that benefit most from AI will likely be the ones that combine strong tools with good training, clear policies, and a culture that is open to innovation.
