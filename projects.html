Featured Final Project

AI Adoption in Construction and Facilities

This final project studied how artificial intelligence is being adopted, implemented, and managed within construction, facilities, healthcare, and AEC-related organizations. The project combined academic research with anonymized interviews from professionals connected to healthcare operations, construction technology, project management, and executive leadership.

The central takeaway was that AI adoption is not just about buying software. It is an organizational change process that depends on governance, user trust, workflow fit, training, data protection, and measurable value. Across the interviews, AI appeared strongest in office-based, document-heavy workflows, while field adoption remains promising but more difficult to scale.

Key Findings From the Final Project

The findings below summarize the main themes that appeared across the literature review, interview data, final report, and presentation.

1

AI Adoption Is Problem-Driven

The strongest use cases were connected to real workflow pain points: meeting notes, document review, internal process questions, safety observations, and specification searches.

2

Governance Comes First

Security, privacy, HIPAA/PHI concerns, proprietary data, and human review were major factors in how organizations selected and implemented AI tools.

3

Office Workflows Benefit First

AI is currently showing clearer value in document-heavy and process-heavy work than in field-side operations where data is messier and errors carry higher consequences.

4

Pilots Build Trust

The organizations studied tended to use committees, pilots, champions, training sessions, and feedback loops before scaling AI tools broadly.

5

Human Review Remains Essential

AI output was treated as support rather than final authority. Clinical, contractual, safety, and field-related outputs still require professional judgment.

6

Future Value Is in Integration

AI agents, reality capture, BIM-to-field comparisons, and internal process automation may become more valuable as organizations improve data quality and governance.

CMET 6240 Safety Project

BIM and Safety

This project explores how Building Information Modeling can help create safer construction sites. The work focuses on how BIM can improve communication, identify hazards earlier, support safety planning, and help project teams better coordinate high-risk construction activities.

The project connects BIM with construction safety by showing how digital models, 4D planning, hazard identification, site logistics, and visual communication can support safer workflows before and during construction.

Additional Project

Togal.AI Demo Results and Estimating Tool Evaluation

This project evaluated Togal.AI as an AI-enabled estimating and takeoff tool using multiple sets of construction drawings. The assignment focused on where the software appeared accurate, where it struggled, and how AI-enabled estimating tools may affect estimating workflows in the future.

My overall conclusion was that Togal.AI has strong potential as an estimating assistant, especially for reducing repetitive takeoff work, but it still requires human review and professional judgment before its output can be trusted in a bid process.

Togal.AI Takeaways

  • Most accurate with door, window, and parking counts.
  • Mostly useful for basic footprint review.
  • Less accurate with net/gross areas, centerlines, and elevation takeoffs.
  • Raises ethical questions around estimating liability and entry-level training.

Project Reflection

My biggest takeaway from these projects is that construction technology only matters when it solves real project problems. Whether the topic is AI adoption, BIM-supported safety, or AI-enabled estimating, the strongest tools are the ones that improve communication, reduce repetitive work, support better decisions, and help teams manage risk.

These projects also helped me connect my interests in project coordination, facilities, BIM/VDC, healthcare construction, safety, and emerging technology. Long-term, I believe construction professionals who understand both the field and digital tools will have an advantage.
